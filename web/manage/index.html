The map

Every document as a circle, sized by how often real answers cited it and coloured by folder; hollow when the document says it is retired. A red line is an open contradiction, a dashed red line two versions of one file, a grey arrow a document retiring the one it names, a green line two documents that answered a question together, an orange square a question asked and not answered. Islands nobody cited are the documents no question has needed yet. Nothing here is inferred: every line is a fact from the panels below, and hovering says which.

Most asked

What people asked most in the last 30 days and how each was answered, counted from the ledger. A question a model answers is paid for every time and can change when the documents do; pinning it makes it free and identical, and the pinned ones say so. One that was refused is also listed by name under “asked and not answered” below. Nobody's name is recorded against any of them.

Asked and not answered

Questions your documents did not fully answer, most asked first — each one a document worth writing, or an answer worth pinning. “Answered in part” means the reply covered some of the question and said plainly that the sources did not cover the rest. That has two causes and this report cannot tell them apart: the section really is missing, or the answer was spread over more of the document than is read at once — a question like “what are all the priorities?” is answered from a few passages, not the whole file. A question stays listed until somebody asks it again, so a gap you have already closed sits here until it is re-asked. Nobody's name is recorded against any of them: the log this reads has no column for who asked.

Answered wrong

Answers readers said were wrong, most-reported first. A refusal shows up above; this is the other failure — an answer that looked exactly like a right one. Stale means the documents changed after it was reported, so it may already be fixed. Nobody's name is recorded here either: what is useful is which answer is wrong and why.

Pinned answers

Answers a person wrote and put their name to — from the lists above, the review queue, or openknowledge pin on the server. A pinned answer is served word for word, at no cost, before any document or model is consulted, so it is also the one answer the grounding check never sees: what is written here is what every reader gets. Edit it here; unpin it and the question goes back to being answered from the documents.

Access

Who may read each folder. A rule names its readers as group:<object-id>, user:<object-id> or authenticated — the ids sign-in provides. The deepest rule wins for its subtree; a folder without one is open to everyone. Answers, the sidebar and the corpus listing all follow the same rule.

Backup

Everything this install would hate to lose, in one file: the pinned answers, the folder access rules, the resolved contradictions, the wrong-answer reports, the ledger — and the documents, unless you leave them out because they already live somewhere you back up. Taken through SQLite's own backup API, so it is safe while questions are being answered. Secrets are not in it: the admin token and any API keys have to be set again after a restore. Restoring is deliberate and stays on the server: openknowledge restore <file>.
