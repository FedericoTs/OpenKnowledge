**"Does a contract worth exactly EUR 25,000 need three quotes?"** The policy
says quotes are required *above* EUR 25,000, and the approval table has a band
reading *EUR 5,001 to EUR 25,000*. Both answers were wrong: the system put the
figure in the band above it and named the wrong approver.

A model shown the right passage still has to read the boundary. This release
tells it how, in one paragraph:

> Read a threshold exactly as the source words it. "Above X" and "more than X"
> do not include X; "up to X", "at least X", and a band written "X to Y"
> include both ends.

The approver question is now right, and right for the stated reason — the
answer says *"the threshold is exactly at EUR 25,000 … for values up to and
including EUR 25,000, the approver is the Head of Department."* That check was
written into the acceptance bar before the run, because an answer that lands on
the right name while still quoting the wrong band is the bug, not a pass.

**Two cases improved and only one of them is claimed.** The second was not
predicted, and it was already giving two different answers to the same
question, so a case that was flipping anyway flipping again is not evidence.
Counting it would have made the headline look better and meant less.

**One case still fails, and it is worth reading.** Asked the same question
about quotes, the answer now reasons correctly — it quotes the band starting at
EUR 25,001, notices 25,000 falls below it, checks the second document, and
concludes *"no three competitive quotes are required."* But it **opens** with
*"Yes, a contract with an annual value of exactly EUR 25,000 needs three
competitive quotes."* It states a guess, then corrects itself five paragraphs
later. Anyone who reads the first line is told the opposite of the answer.
Making the model answer after reasoning rather than before is a separate
change, and this release makes one change.

**A citation that was not real, found inside the answer this release fixed.**
The correct approver answer quotes what looks like a row of the policy table:

> "Contract value (annual): Above EUR 25,000 | Approver: Chief Financial
> Officer | Additional requirement: Three competitive quotes"

There is no such row. "Above EUR 25,000" appears in the document as prose about
quotes and is never paired with an approver; the answer welded it to a
different row and presented the result in quotation marks. The conclusion drawn
from it is correct and the invented row is even true in substance — which is
what makes it hard to catch. The grounding check measures word and number
overlap, and every word in that row is real somewhere in the document, so it
sees nothing wrong.

That hole is not new and it is not fixed here: the same check passed an
invented act of a three-act play in v0.12.8. Verifying that a quotation is a
quotation is its own change, with its own risk of rejecting honest paraphrase.
It is recorded rather than rushed.

Nothing in this release changes what a plain fact question does. Cached answers
are invalidated: the prompt moved v5 to v6.
